
# Case 03: Motor Will Not Start
**Case Reference:** SIM-MOTOR-003
> **Fictional support simulation**
>

## Case Status

**Draft — unresolved; additional evidence required.**

No failed component has been confirmed. No repair has been performed or verified.

## Scenario Overview

A customer reports that the electric motor on an industrial hydraulic power unit will not start when the normal start command is issued.

According to the fictional customer report, the equipment operated during the previous shift. The operator has not provided a verified alarm record, control-system status, or a detailed account of what changed before the problem appeared.

The immediate support objective is to clarify what "will not start" means, document available information, and prepare an appropriate escalation without directing unsafe hands-on troubleshooting.

## Reported Symptoms

The following statements are synthetic customer reports, not independently verified findings:

| Reported information | Evidence status |
|---|---|
| The motor does not run after the normal start command. | Customer report; not independently verified. |

| No unusual sound was reported during the attempted start. | Customer report; observation conditions are unknown. |
| No alarm text or fault code has been supplied. | Information gap; this does not establish that no fault exists. |

These reports do not establish whether the issue involves the start request, a required permissive, the control system, electrical equipment, the motor, or the driven machinery.

## Support Objective

Demonstrate a structured remote-support response that:

- Clarifies the customer's description of the symptom.
- Separates reported observations from verified evidence.
- Identifies missing documentation and operating context.
- Communicates uncertainty without guessing at a failed part.
- Routes the issue to the appropriate qualified personnel.
- Defines what evidence would be needed before closing the case.

## Initial Intake Questions

The initial customer communication will request information already available through normal records, documentation, and authorized operator interfaces:

1. What are the equipment manufacturer, model, and asset ID?
2. What exactly happens when the normal start command is issued?
3. Does the motor remain still, briefly attempt to move, or start and then stop?
4. What messages, indicators, or fault codes are shown on the normal operator interface?
5. When did the equipment last operate normally?
6. Was maintenance, a power interruption, a shutdown, or a configuration change reported before the symptom appeared?
7. Is the issue limited to this equipment, or have other affected systems been reported?
8. Are the applicable operator manual, drawings, and recent maintenance records available?

These questions are requests for existing information. They do not authorize repeated start attempts, fault resets, access to electrical enclosures, or hands-on testing.

## Evidence Boundaries

At this stage, the case contains no verified:

- Electrical measurements.
- Motor or starter inspection results.
- Control-system event logs.
- Confirmation of required start permissives.
- Mechanical inspection findings.
- Manufacturer-specific diagnostic results.

A reported illuminated indicator is not treated as proof that all required power or control conditions are present.

The absence of a supplied fault code is not treated as proof that the equipment has no recorded fault.

## Scope of Remote Support

### Included

- Customer intake and symptom clarification.
- Review of supplied documentation and records.
- Assessment of evidence quality and missing information.
- Customer-facing status communication.
- Preparation of an internal engineering escalation.

### Excluded

- Remote authorization to return equipment to service.
- Instructions to open electrical enclosures.
- Energized electrical testing.
- Bypassing interlocks, permissives, or protective devices.
- Repeated start attempts or protective-device resets.
- Component replacement without an approved diagnosis.
- Claims that the motor or another component has failed.

Any hands-on inspection or testing is outside this portfolio exercise and would require appropriately qualified personnel, manufacturer instructions, and applicable site safety procedures.
