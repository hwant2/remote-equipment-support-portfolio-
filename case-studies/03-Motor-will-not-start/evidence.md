## 1. Purpose

This document organizes the available synthetic customer
information concerning an industrial hydraulic power unit
whose electric motor reportedly does not start.

The objective is to:

- Record the available customer-reported information.
- Identify which observations remain unverified.
- Distinguish possible fault categories from confirmed findings.
- Identify missing records and technical documentation.
- Establish the evidence needed for further assessment.
- Support an appropriate engineering escalation.

The information currently available is insufficient to
identify a failed component or establish a root cause.

## 4. Evidence Classification

For this assessment, information is divided into four
categories.

### A. Customer-Reported Information

Statements supplied by the fictional customer that have
not been independently verified.

Examples:

- The motor does not run after a start request.
- The equipment operated during the previous shift.
- An unidentified panel indicator appears illuminated.

### B. Scenario-Defined Information

Information established only for the educational simulation.

Examples:

- The equipment is an industrial hydraulic power unit.
- An electric motor drives the hydraulic system.
- The issue is being assessed through a remote-support workflow.

Scenario-defined information is not evidence from an
actual installation.

### C. Missing Evidence

Information required to narrow the investigation but
not currently available.

Examples:

- Control-system event records.
- Equipment electrical drawings.
- Motor and starter specifications.
- Applicable manufacturer documentation.
- Relevant maintenance and operating records.

### D. Working Hypotheses

Technically plausible explanations requiring supporting
evidence before any conclusion can be reached.

Hypotheses must not be presented as verified findings.

## 5. Symptom Definition

The phrase "motor will not start" is not sufficiently
specific for a component-level diagnosis.

Different reported behaviors could represent different
technical conditions.

| Possible symptom description | Current status |

## 6. Working Technical Hypotheses

The following categories provide a structured basis for
further evidence review.

No category is currently preferred or confirmed.

### H-01 — Start Request or Control-Command Issue

**Hypothesis:** The normal start request may not be
recognized or processed as expected.

**Supporting evidence:** None currently available.

**Evidence needed:**

- Applicable control-system description.
- Existing operator-interface records.
- Available event history.
- Authorized documentation describing start-command behavior.

**Status:** Unverified.

### H-02 — Start Permissive or Protective Inhibition

**Hypothesis:** A required operating condition or
protective function may be preventing the start sequence.

**Supporting evidence:** None currently available.

**Evidence needed:**

- Manufacturer-defined start permissives.
- Existing alarm and event records.
- Relevant operating-state documentation.
- Available maintenance history.

**Status:** Unverified.

No bypassing or overriding of permissives or protective
functions is authorized or recommended.

### H-03 — Electrical Supply or Motor-Control Equipment

**Hypothesis:** A problem involving the electrical
supply or motor-control equipment may affect starting.

**Supporting evidence:** None currently available.

**Evidence needed:**

- Electrical system documentation.
- Motor and starter specifications.
- Available historical fault records.
- Findings from qualified personnel using approved
  procedures, if subsequently authorized.

**Status:** Unverified.

No electrical measurements or inspection results are
available in this simulation.

### H-04 — Electric Motor Condition

**Hypothesis:** A motor-related condition may prevent

## 8. Initial Evidence Assessment

The available information supports only the following
limited statements:

1. A fictional customer has reported that an electric
   motor does not run after a normal start command.
2. The equipment reportedly operated during the
   previous shift.
3. A control-panel indicator was reportedly illuminated,
   but its identity and meaning are unknown.
4. No usable alarm history, electrical documentation,
   or verified diagnostic information has been supplied.
5. The reported symptoms do not support a confirmed
   electrical, control-system, motor, or mechanical failure.

The available evidence is insufficient for a
component-specific recommendation.

## 9. Information Gaps and Technical Uncertainty

The assessment is limited by the absence of:

- Verified equipment identification.
- Motor and starter specifications.
- Control-system architecture.
- Manufacturer-defined start permissives.
- Existing event and alarm records.
- Verified operating timeline.
- Recent maintenance documentation.
- Authorized technical findings.

Without this information, the relationship between
the reported symptom and any specific component
remains unknown.

The investigation should not be narrowed solely
because one indicator reportedly appears illuminated
or no alarm code has been supplied.

## 10. Remote-Support Safety Boundaries

This document does not authorize or instruct anyone to:

- Open electrical enclosures.
- Perform energized electrical measurements.
- Reset protective devices.
- Repeat unsuccessful start attempts.
- Bypass interlocks or start permissives.
- Force control-system outputs.
- Disconnect electrical or hydraulic components.
- Replace a motor, starter, or other component.
- Return equipment to service.

Requests are limited to existing documentation,
historical records, and information available through
normal authorized operator interfaces.

Any hands-on inspection, diagnostic testing, repair,
or return-to-service decision requires appropriately
qualified and authorized personnel following applicable
manufacturer instructions and site safety procedures.
