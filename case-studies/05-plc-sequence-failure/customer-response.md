## 2. Simulated Customer Email

*The following email is fictional correspondence prepared for portfolio demonstration. It has not been sent.*

**Subject:** Technical Support Follow-Up — Intermittent PLC Sequence Interruption | SIM-PLC-005

Dear Customer,

Thank you for providing the initial information regarding the intermittent operating sequence interruption affecting your automated equipment.

Based on the reported symptoms, we understand that the machine begins its normal operating sequence but occasionally fails to advance to the next programmed operating stage. We also understand that some production cycles reportedly complete normally.

The available information does not currently establish whether the interruption involves an unsatisfied operating condition, inconsistent sensor feedback, PLC input or output behavior, mechanical equipment response, control logic, industrial communications, or an applicable protective function.

A sequence interruption does not necessarily indicate a failed PLC. The control system may be responding as designed to an operating condition that has not been satisfied.

To support a more focused technical assessment, we would appreciate the following existing information, where available through approved documentation and authorized personnel.

### Equipment and Control-System Identification

- Equipment manufacturer, model, and asset identification.
- PLC manufacturer, processor model, and applicable hardware configuration.
- Programming software, firmware, and documented control program revision information.

### Technical Documentation

- Applicable electrical and control-system drawings.
- Approved machine sequence descriptions and control narratives.
- Documented operating permissive and interlock requirements.

### Operating History and Diagnostic Records

- Description of when the interruption occurs and the affected operating stage, if known.
- Existing PLC input and output state records associated with the reported condition.
- Existing HMI alarm and event history.
- Available mechanical and actuator response records.
- Relevant maintenance and approved configuration change history.
- Existing industrial communication and remote I/O records, if applicable.

### Existing Technical Findings

- Previously documented findings from appropriately qualified and authorized personnel, if available.

The requested information will help determine whether further controls, electrical, instrumentation, mechanical, or industrial communication review would be appropriate.

At this stage, the available information does not support a recommendation to replace the PLC, modify the control program, adjust timer settings, or change protective-function configurations.

Please ensure that any equipment inspection, controller access, electrical testing, programming activity, or maintenance intervention is performed only by appropriately qualified and authorized personnel following manufacturer documentation and applicable site safety procedures.

This information request does not authorize changes to equipment operating conditions or protective functions.

Once the relevant existing information becomes available, the proposed next step would be to assess evidence completeness and determine the appropriate scope of qualified technical review.

We appreciate your assistance and look forward to supporting the proposed technical assessment.

Respectfully,

Technical Support  
Remote Equipment Support Portfolio

## 6. Technical Expectations and Limitations

The customer should not interpret the preliminary assessment as confirmation of:

- PLC processor failure.
- Incorrect control logic.
- Failed input or output modules.
- Sensor failure.
- Actuator failure.
- Industrial communication failure.
- Incorrect timer configuration.
- Protective-function or interlock malfunction.
- A specific unsatisfied sequence transition condition.

An interrupted sequence may represent intended controller behavior when an applicable operating condition is not satisfied.

A recorded PLC input state does not independently establish actual mechanical position.

A recorded PLC output command does not independently establish successful equipment movement.

The available information does not support a component replacement recommendation, programming modification, or equipment return-to-service decision.

No repair timeline, engineering approval, or guaranteed technical outcome is represented.
