# Case 01: Hydraulic Pressure Drops Under Load

**Case Reference:** SIM-HYD-001  
**Scenario:** Fictional educational simulation  
**Data Source:** Synthetic customer observations  
**Documentation Status:** Four-Document Case Package Complete  
**Technical Status:** Unresolved — Additional Evidence Required


## 1. Customer Complaint

"The hydraulic unit starts normally, but the cylinder slows down
and stops when we apply the load. The motor keeps running, and
we do not see any external leaks. It seemed to work normally
earlier in the week."

## 2. Support Objective

Organize a safe investigation and identify the evidence needed
to distinguish among a load-related problem, an unintended
fluid-flow path, and a hydraulic-supply problem.

The objective is not to identify a failed component from the
customer complaint alone.

## 3. Fictional System Description

For this exercise, the system contains:

- Electric motor and hydraulic pump
- Reservoir and filtration
- Main pressure-relief valve
- Directional-control valve
- Double-acting cylinder
- Installed pressure gauge

The circuit drawing and component specifications have not
been supplied. Pressure settings and component ratings
are not assumed.

## 4. Safety and Scope

This case is not a maintenance procedure.

Hands-on inspection, testing, isolation, and repair require
appropriately qualified personnel using manufacturer
instructions and applicable site procedures.

This remote review does not authorize:

- Adjusting pressure settings
- Disconnecting hydraulic lines
- Searching for leaks by hand
- Bypassing guards or interlocks
- Opening energized electrical equipment
- Performing unapproved loaded tests

Intrusive testing requires escalation to authorized
on-site personnel.

## 5. Evidence Summary

The customer reports unloaded movement but stalling under load.
The symptom reportedly began after maintenance.

The load, pressure indication, circuit configuration, and
maintenance details remain unverified.

[Review the supporting evidence](evidence.md)

## 6. Working Hypotheses

These are possibilities, not confirmed diagnoses:

1. The load or driven mechanism has changed.
2. Fluid is taking an unintended path within the circuit.
3. The hydraulic supply is not meeting the operating requirement.
4. Recent maintenance changed system behavior.
5. The pressure indication is misleading.

## 7. Investigation Decision

The current evidence does not justify identifying a failed
component, replacing the pump, or adjusting a valve.

Request the circuit drawing, maintenance record, and results
from the manufacturer-approved diagnostic procedure.
